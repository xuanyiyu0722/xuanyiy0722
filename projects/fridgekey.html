FridgeKey

Smart Fridge Lockers - Make Shared Living Environment More Accountable

This project identifies common problems with shared refrigerators in shared living environments, including unclear food ownership, limited storage organization, and difficulty managing shared space. Inspired by the locker system, FridgeKey extends the idea of individual lockers into a shared refrigerator, creating designated and secure storage for each resident. The system combines physical compartments with digital access and management tools to make shared refrigerator use more organized, traceable, and accountable.

01 / Context & desk research

Background

Food theft is a recurring issue in shared refrigerators. I reviewed reports and discussion posts to understand the problem across dormitories, shared kitchens, and workplaces.

Dorm food theft — shared pain points

Three recurring problems make shared food storage difficult to manage.

01 / Ownership

Clearly labeled food still gets taken

Labels identify a name, but do little to prevent food from being taken. Students also reported that RAs have limited power to intervene.

02 / Access

A personal fridge isn’t a real solution

A mini fridge may seem like an easy workaround, but it is not affordable for everyone. Many high school dormitories also prohibit private refrigerators.

“How do I stop people from eating my clearly labeled food from the communal fridge?”

“Ask a parent to help you obtain a mini fridge? Ask a roommate if y’all could split costs on a fridge? Ask a friend with a fridge if you could leave your food in there?”
03 / Accountability

Responsibility is hard to identify

Without evidence, food theft becomes difficult to investigate. Residents struggle to identify who is responsible or resolve the issue.

“This girl would steal my food then blame it on the neighbors or tell me ‘no you only bought x amount’.”

“Don’t room with someone just because you went to the same high school and they were cool from what little hanging out you guys do. Ask me how I know… This girl would steal my food then blame it on the neighbors or tell me ‘no you only bought x amount’.”

Overall key takeaway

Shared food needs clear ownership.

Food theft in shared refrigerators is not simply a storage problem. Even when food is clearly labeled, users still lack a reliable way to establish ownership, control access, and identify responsibility.

06 / Evaluating existing solutions

Competitor Analysis

I compared physical locks, personal containers, smart refrigerators, and compartment-based storage to identify the gap in shared refrigerator use.

↗

Refrigerator Lock

Core function

Locks the entire refrigerator physically.

Weakness

Treats the refrigerator as a single private unit. It works well for individual households, but is not designed for shared environments and cannot establish personal ownership within a shared space.

↗

Padlock Lunchbox

Core function

Uses a physical lock to secure food inside a personal container.

Weakness

Stores a limited amount of food. It does not organize shared refrigerator space, track freshness, or provide access records.

↗

Bluetooth Refrigerator Lock

Core function

Allows users to lock and unlock the refrigerator through a mobile app, offering more flexible control than a traditional lock.

Weakness

Still treats the refrigerator as a single unit, rather than supporting individual access within a shared space.

↗

Samsung Smart Refrigerator

Core function

Uses AI vision and internal cameras to help track food. Users can view contents, manage food lists, and connect through an app. Related convenience features include Auto Open Door, Dual Ice Maker, and a door alarm.

Weakness

Focuses on household food visibility and kitchen convenience. It does not solve food ownership in shared environments, and the price is high.

↗

Fridge with internal lockers

Core function

Provides individual locked compartments to prevent food theft and establish clear ownership in a shared refrigerator.

Weakness

Focuses on secure storage without food tracking or monitoring. Opaque lockers also make it harder for users to find their items.

Conclusion

Connect ownership, access, visibility, and accountability.

Existing solutions either lock the entire refrigerator, protect only a small personal container, or focus on food tracking without solving ownership in shared spaces. A better system should combine personal ownership, flexible access control, food visibility, and activity tracking.

16 / From prototype to production

Cost

The estimated production cost is approximately $42 per smart fridge locker. Sourcing, assembly, and modular sizing provide a path toward a lower unit cost at scale.

Strategy

  • Source hardware from China to reduce component costs and take advantage of lower manufacturing prices.
  • Vary cost by SKU and size, since different locker dimensions require different enclosures.
  • Complete final assembly in the U.S. for easier quality control, testing, maintenance, and deployment.
