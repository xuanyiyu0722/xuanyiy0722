MUSUBI

Connecting Traditional Garment with BJD Clothing Customization

MUSUBI simplifies the traditional clothing customization process for BJD owners by bringing scattered resources and steps into one platform. From learning about garment types and finding suitable fabrics to discovering makers, checking patterns, and tracking customization progress, the platform creates a clearer and more connected experience for completing traditional BJD clothing.

Background

01 / Ball-Jointed Dolls

What is a BJD?

The doll market has a long history. Today, Ball-Jointed Dolls (BJDs) are one of the most recognizable types of articulated dolls. They are named for their ball-shaped joints, which allow the body to move and hold a wide range of poses. The broader doll market also includes other forms such as Mechanically Jointed Dolls (MJD), bisque dolls, hina dolls, etc.

Introduce Kimono

Kimono is a traditional Japanese garment. The classic form of a kimono has a T-shaped, straight-cut construction. It is wrapped around the body and secured with a sash called an obi. Kimono come in many types for different occasions, levels of formality, ages, and seasons. Their fabrics, sleeve lengths, colors, motifs, and accessories can also communicate different meanings.

I list several common kimono types here, arranged from
more formal on the left to less formal on the right.

Iro-Uchikake

色打掛（いろうちかけ）

Iro-Uchikake is a richly decorated outer wedding kimono worn by brides. Unlike the all-white Shiromuku, it features vivid colors, embroidery, and symbolic motifs such as cranes, flowers, and seasonal patterns.

Furisode

振袖（ふりそで）

Furisode is a formal kimono recognized by its long, flowing sleeves. It is traditionally associated with unmarried young women and is often worn for Coming-of-Age ceremonies, weddings, and other celebrations.

Kurotomesode

黒留袖（くろとめそで）

Kurotomesode is one of the most formal kimono for married women. It has a black base with decorative motifs concentrated around the lower part of the garment and is often worn at formal family events such as weddings.

Hōmongi

訪問着（ほうもんぎ）

Hōmongi is a semi-formal kimono whose decorative patterns often continue across seams, creating one connected composition. It is suitable for visits, parties, weddings, and other social occasions.

Tsukesage

付下げ（つけさげ）

Tsukesage is a semi-formal kimono with more restrained decoration than Hōmongi. Its motifs are arranged so that they appear upright when the garment is worn, making it appropriate for many social and formal occasions.

Hakama

袴（はかま）

Hakama are pleated traditional trousers or a skirt-like lower garment worn over kimono. They are associated with formal ceremonies, martial arts, and graduation attire, and are worn by both men and women.

Iromuji

色無地（いろむじ）

Iromuji is a single-colored kimono without large decorative patterns. Its simple appearance makes it flexible for different settings, and its level of formality can change depending on the presence of family crests and accessories.

Komon

小紋（こもん）

Komon is a casual kimono covered with small repeated patterns across the fabric. It is commonly worn for everyday outings, casual gatherings, and informal social occasions.

03 / Custom Commission

The BJD Kimono Customization Market

Kimono customization is a common practice in the BJD community. Collectors often prepare their own fabric, usually by repurposing a full-size kimono, and commission a maker to create kimono for a specific doll size. The process involves many steps, including understanding kimono styles, sourcing fabrics, finding experienced makers, confirming doll measurements, and communicating customization details. For first-time customers, this process is complicated and time-consuming.

What is the most difficult part of commissioning BJD kimono?

  1. Understanding kimono types
  2. Finding suitable fabric
  3. Knowing fabric requirements
  4. Communicating with the maker

Kimono Familiarity: The overall BJD community has relatively low familiarity with kimono.

Purchase Experience: Most respondents have never purchased kimono for their dolls.

Purchase Method: Among buyers, custom-made kimono is slightly more common than ready-made options.

Commission Effort: The commissioning process is generally perceived as time-consuming and demanding.

Fabric Splitting: Most respondents are willing to split fabric if it helps reduce the cost.

Main Problem: The main challenges involve kimono knowledge, fabric sourcing, material requirements, and maker communication.

“One full-size kimono provides much more fabric than I need. I usually have around fabric left after making one 1/3-scale kimono depending on the pattern. It is difficult to deal with all the leftovers, so I just keep them stored away.”
“I wanted to customize a kimono for my c’s BJD. But I had no idea how this worked at first. I spent a time researching on Xiaohongshu and searching for suitable fabric in livestreams. Finding a maker and waiting for production then took several more months.”

The research shows that people think getting started with BJD kimono customization is complicated. The study suggests a need for a more integrated and beginner-friendly customization process that connects kimono knowledge, fabric sourcing, fabric splitting, and maker communication in one place.

Name

I name this app

MUSUBI 「結」

A Japanese word meaning connection, tying together, or the knot of the obi.

This name connects the app’s purpose and cultural focus:

  1. 1It represents the app’s goal of connecting traditional garment culture, BJD collectors, fabrics, and makers through one shared platform.
  2. 2It is inspired by Japanese culture, since kimono is the platform’s primary garment focus, making a Japanese name especially fitting for the app.

Hannah’s first doll kimono commission

Interested

Read the seven-step journey

  1. Interested

    Hannah sees another collector’s BJD wearing a kimono on social media and wants to create one for her own doll.

  2. Explore

    She explores kimono types, styling rules, and accessories to understand what would suit her SDGr doll.

  3. Find Fabric

    She filters the Materials section to find fabrics that match her preferred kimono type, budget, and style.

  4. Plan & Split

    She checks the recommended fabric amount for her doll size, selects the sections she needs, and joins a fabric split with other users.

  5. Find Maker

    She compares makers by doll size, kimono type, portfolio, pricing, and availability, then invites a maker to the project.

  6. Track Progress

    She follows the commission from the maker’s acceptance and measurements to fabric shipment, making, and preview.

  7. Receive & Share

    She receives the finished kimono, dresses her doll, and shares the result on social media.

Future Enhancement

01

Expand Pattern & Fabric Types

Beyond kimono, expand traditional garment patterns and materials.

  • e.g. Hanfu, Saree, Dirndl, Victorian dress.
  • e.g. Song brocade, French lace, silk satin.
  • Help users explore and customize a wider range of traditional garments.
  • Provide size, usage, and compatibility information for each material.

02

Finished Kimono Marketplace

Create a space for users to resell or auction completed BJD kimono they no longer use.

  • Supports secondhand circulation.
  • Gives handmade garments a longer life cycle.
  • Helps collectors discover unique finished pieces.

03

Maker-Owned Fabrics

Allow makers to display fabrics they already own and are available to use for future commissions.

  • Users can choose materials directly from a maker's collection.
  • Reduces the source fabric step.
  • Provide a channel for makers to consume materials they already bought.

04

Fabric-Led Commission Posts

Makers can post a specific fabric as an open commission opportunity.

  • Turns leftover or special fabrics into new commission opportunities.
  • Helps makers attract users based on specific materials.
  • Creates a more direct connection between fabric, maker, and collector.
